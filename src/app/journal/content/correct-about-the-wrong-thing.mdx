A print-mastering chain that takes a 1024×1280 original to 4800×6000 — exactly
300 DPI at 16×20 — is mostly a stack of measurements standing on each other. On
2026-09-24 five of those measurements were caught giving wrong answers, and the
chain shipped nothing.

Not one of the five had a bug. Each computed its own quantity correctly and
reported it honestly. **The failure mode worth naming is that an instrument
rarely fails by computing wrong — it fails by computing correctly about the
wrong thing.** A proxy for a harm is not the harm, and from inside the number
the difference does not show.

Then a human eye at 1:1 rejected a file that had passed all five corrected
gates, which is the part of the day that earned the other five.

## A small RSS is the thrash

The chain hung, and nothing on a 38 GB machine looked guilty. The largest
process held 0.6 GB and the kernel called 58% of memory free.

The guilty process was the one reading 0.61 GB. A ComfyUI session accumulates
allocations across sequential prompt submissions, and this one was holding
roughly 10 GB of them — swapped out, not resident. RSS measures residency, so a
session reading itself back from swap on every inference reports as a small,
innocent process, and hunting the memory hog by RSS sends you looking at
unrelated apps.

**The reading inverts, which is why the instinct fails.** Measured across the
same lane's failed and fixed runs, ComfyUI's RSS was 0.61 GB while thrashing
and 3.98 GB while healthy — swap at 22994 MB against 15903 MB, per-tile time 50
seconds and climbing against 44 seconds and steady. **A large RSS is the
healthy state**, because it means the model is resident. The ordinary instinct
to suspect whichever process holds the most memory points at the bench that is
working and away from the one that is dying.

One HTTP call to ComfyUI's `/free` released 9.5 GB of swap. Diagnosis and cure
are the same call, which is the useful property: if swap drops sharply
afterwards, the session was the cause.

## Three proxies, one harm

Three guards for memory pressure were convicted on this bench inside one
campaign, each failing in a different direction.

**Pageouts per second slept through a real thrash.** The stop-line was ruled at
sustained pageouts above 100/s. It read 0/s while frame 1 took 431 seconds
against a 224-second baseline and frame 2 never landed at all, with swap
sitting at 18.9 of 19.4 GB.

**Swap occupancy would have aborted a healthy bench.** macOS grows its
encrypted swap file on demand and then keeps it near-full by design, so
occupancy read 94% — 16348 of 17408 MB — on a bench with 0 swapouts/s, 0
pageouts/s, 67% of memory free by the kernel's own count, and three consecutive
tiles landing at 44, 44 and 44 seconds. A bench turning out three identical
tiles is not thrashing, and occupancy near 100% is the steady state of a grown
swap file rather than evidence of pressure.

**Swapouts per second fired on a run that was fine.** Set at 200/s, it tripped
on tile 4 of a healthy run at 278/s while every tile was landing on time.

The third is the instructive one, because it was written into the same class
whose docstring already said swapouts are reported and never ruled on alone. A
threshold picked without measuring what normal looks like is the exact error
that class exists to correct, committed one screen below the paragraph
correcting it.

**The ruling: guard on the harm itself.** The damage a stop-line exists to
prevent is a job that stops making progress, and that is directly observable as
a tile whose wall-clock blows past the run's own warm median. The abort sits at
2.5× that median and never rules on fewer than 3 warm samples, with swapouts and
occupancy printed beside it and deciding nothing. A run's own warm median is a
ruler the run calibrates for itself, which is why it survives a change of
canvas, of machine and of subject.

That replacement was then ratified while still blind to the case it had been
built citing, because it evaluated after a tile completed and a tile that never
completes never reaches the check. It now bounds the wait rather than only
inspecting the result: a tile that has not returned within 6× the warm median,
floored at 300 seconds, is interrupted and aborted, so a hang costs about five
minutes instead of an unbounded stretch of night. Two owners there, and both are
worth naming — the guard was built without asking when it ran, and it was
ratified without that question being asked either. **When a check runs is part of
what the check is**, and a guard specified only by its threshold is
half-specified.

## A watch armed on events cannot see their absence

The monitoring around the run was entirely event-driven — a log tail, and waits
armed on strings like `done in`. A hang produces no line to match, so the tail
stayed quiet, the waits stayed armed, and the quiet was indistinguishable from
a bench that simply had not printed yet. 106 minutes passed between checks on
the belief that no news was good news.

The replacement watches mtime instead of content. A working run advances its
log on a cadence the work does not control and a hung one does not, so
staleness is a positive, checkable fact rather than an inference from silence,
and it costs one `stat()` to read.

**It nearly shipped unable to fail.** The first attempt to prove it could
detect a stall used the killed pilot's own log, and the watchdog reported the
file alive — killing the run had touched the file's mtime. A watchdog validated
against a file that was not stale would have passed its own test and then
watched all night without the ability to fire. It was re-proved against a
synthetic 98-minute-stale file before it was trusted.

## A ratio is not a threshold

The fourth instrument was an invention ratio: leaked high-frequency energy over
the high-frequency headroom available in the original, with a 5% gate on it.
Stage 1 of the pilot tripped that gate at 10.2% and the run halted.

The gate was wrong, and the proof was already in the house's own records. Run
at the ratified mask floor against a master this house had accepted and
shipped, the same measure reads **5.2%** — the gate sat a hair below the work it
was meant to protect, and nothing had noticed because it had only ever been
read on two pieces sitting close together on one curve.

The structural reason is that a ratio's denominator moves with the subject and
with position on the ladder. On the pilot, stage 0 read 1.8% at an absolute
ground delta of 0.455 LSB while stage 1 read 10.2% at 0.366 LSB. **The ratio
rose 5.7× while the actual difference fell.** A metric whose measuring point
moves with its subject is not a measurement.

So the ratio was demoted to a permanent tripwire, which is work it does well —
it forced a stop-and-look that afternoon and the stop was correct even though
the number was not. Shippability moved to the absolute ground delta against a
frozen 0.79 LSB visibility threshold, and a frame that needs a new threshold in
order to pass fails instead.

## The frame passed every gate and failed the eye

The master then built clean. 4800×6000 exactly, per-rung ground deltas of
0.455, 0.366 and 0.349 LSB all inside the frozen threshold, seam audit passing,
ground pristine, and every tile landing on cadence including the one where the
previous run had hung.

At 1:1 the charcoal read as pale-outlined cellular clumping with an olive-blue
cast — a diffusion model's idea of granularity rather than charcoal sitting in
paper tooth. Chroma in the darks ran 4.12 → 7.45 → 10.09 → 12.09, injected
monotonically at every rung.

**The mask did its job, and the damage was where it opened — the one place
nothing was measuring.** Every invention check this lane had built watched the
ground, and the ground was the part that was fine.

The mechanism is that the mask has a floor and no ceiling. The floor says
_below this, variation is the ground's own facture, so leave it._ Nothing said
_above this, variation is the stroke's own facture, so leave that too._ And a
local-contrast mask opens widest exactly where a mark is densest: this piece's
stroke body sits at local contrast p50 18.5 against a ratified `abs_hi` of
18.49, so the mask is saturated fully open across 86% of the stroke body. That
one fact predicts every observation — pristine ground, overwritten stroke
interior, damage scaling with total refine.

The precedent could not have caught it. The chain was validated on a
continuous-tone piece, which has no stroke interior to damage and where chroma
in the darks is expected, and it met a mark-making register for the first time
here. That is the same shape as the ratio: a constant derived from two subjects
sitting close together on a curve, broken by a third.

## The obvious fix, measured dead in five minutes

A ceiling on the mask is the obvious repair, and it does not work. Establishing
that cost five minutes of CPU and no GPU at all, because the ceiling is a
composite-time parameter and the refined tiles were already on disk.

Recomposed on the same refined tiles, the trade-off runs one way the whole
distance. With no ceiling the master keeps 83.1% of the restoration at a
roughness of 13.00 against the un-refined control's 12.12; at a ceiling of 20 it
keeps 51.3% at 12.58; at a ceiling of 13 the roughness finally matches the
control exactly at 12.12 and the restoration kept has collapsed to 7.9%.
**Register reaches the un-refined control only at the setting where the refine
has stopped working, so register and restoration trade about 1:1.**

The reason outlives the parameter. **Local contrast cannot distinguish a
stroke's own facture from the edge detail worth restoring, because they are the
same measurement.** The floor works because smooth ground genuinely is
separable on that axis. The top end is not, so no one-dimensional gate there
can do better than swap one for the other at par.

The parameter shipped anyway — validated byte-identical when disabled, with the
earlier accepted master still reproducing through it — so the next lane to
reach for the obvious idea finds the measurement that rules it out instead of
spending a day rediscovering it. A negative result is cheaper to ship than to
repeat.

## The instrument that caught it could not fail at first either

None of these replacements were trusted on the strength of passing, because a
check that cannot fail is not a check — and the instrument that finally measured
this defect needed two corrections before it could fail at all. Its first version
counted single-pixel luminance
maxima and read zero on both the failing master and its control, because at 4800
px wide the cells are 10 to 20 px across and a pixel-level peak test cannot see
them. Its second fault was the control: compared against its own rung's control,
which already carries every earlier rung's refinement, the failing master reads
1.06× on roughness and looks fine, while against the pure-lanczos ladder it
reads 1.27× — the real, cumulative damage. **The first reading would have cleared
a picture the eye had already rejected.**

## What the file is actually for

The question that reframed the standard came from outside the instruments
entirely. Do we want to be inventing textures when the textures will be printed
on?

No. The physical stock has its own surface at its own scale and the print gets
it for free, so a depicted interior texture is unwanted twice over: it is
invented, and it competes with the paper's own tooth at a different scale,
leaving two granularities in the picture and neither of them the paper's. **The
file's job is the drawing; the paper's job is the paper.**

That settles what a 1:1 crop cannot: between a softer interior and a crisper
invented one the softer wins, because the paper finishes it. And a physical proof
on the actual stock now enters the acceptance path before any master is approved,
because nothing on this bench can see what paper does.

## The anchor is why the eye could rule

Five corrected instruments are why the eye got a clean question to answer. The
rejected master stayed on disk as an anchor, the lanczos-only control sat beside
it, and the second variant was built without discarding the first — so the
judgment was this against that, at 1:1, in the same window of the same picture.

**An eye with nothing to compare against negotiates.** Shown one picture it
asks whether the picture is good enough, which is a question about patience.
Shown two it asks which one is better, which is a question about the pictures.

A pre-authorised overnight run of eight frames was refused that night because
nothing had yet passed an eye. Every one of the five corrections narrowed what
the numbers could hide, and the defect that stopped the work was still found by
looking.
